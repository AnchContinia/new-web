The run by hand

Four jobs, every single run.

None of them is hard. All of them are somebody's afternoon, repeated on whatever cycle your provider expects — and every one of them happens outside the ledger the invoices came from.

  1. Find the invoices. Filter open sales invoices by customer, date and amount, decide which ones qualify, and copy them out.
  2. Rebuild them in the provider's template. Their columns are not your columns. The mapping lives in somebody's head, and it is redone from scratch each time.
  3. Send the file and hope it lands. Email or a portal upload. Nothing about it is recorded next to the invoices it was built from.
  4. Remember which invoices are theirs now. Nothing in the ledger says an invoice has been factored — until someone puts it there, by hand, without missing one.

Proposal, allocation, file. Inside Business Central.

Continia Finance's Factoring module simplifies receivables factoring from proposals through customer allocation and data files. Three steps, one chain, run against the invoices themselves.

The proposal

Open sales invoices are gathered into a factoring proposal on your own criteria, with the amounts totalled where you can see them.

The customer allocation

Each invoice is allocated to the customer it belongs to, so the proposal reads the same way your receivables do.

The data file

The run produces the data file your factoring provider expects — generated from the proposal, not rebuilt beside it.

Factoring is one of the add-on modules in Continia Finance, which means it installs as part of an extension to Business Central rather than as a system next to it.

What never happens

Four things you will not be doing.

  • Keeping a factoring spreadsheet. The proposal is a record in Business Central, not a file on somebody's desktop.
  • Typing invoice lines a second time. The lines are already in the ledger. The run reads them from there.
  • Keeping a separate list of what has been factored. The allocation sits with the invoices, so there is one place to look instead of two.
  • Signing into somewhere else to do it. Continia Finance is an extension to Business Central — same sign-in, same session, same user.

Part of Continia Finance.

Factoring is not a product of its own. It is one of the modules inside Continia Finance, so it arrives with the rest of the financial tooling and under the same permissions.
